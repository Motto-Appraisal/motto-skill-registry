
# MODE B: AS-IS + SUBJECT-TO (as-renovated / ARV) ORDERS
Use ONLY when the order has a purchase + renovation scope / ARV / "subject-to" / as-completed value (scope PDF present, two valuation groups: as-is comps + renovated comps). This file is independent of `mode_as_is_only.md`: edit one without touching the other. Shared setup, tooling, review, export and filing steps live in SKILL.md.

Price-band note (user 2026-10-16): the old "+/-15% price band" is NOT a hard gate anywhere. Purchase $/sf is the TARGET for the as-is side only; price band, if mentioned below, is a tie-breaker. Never drop a comp that looks like the best match solely because of price; photo-check it.

## B1. Pick 3 as-is + 3 as-renovated (the "3+3")
- AS-IS SIDE (revised 2026-10-04 after 2017 Jennifer Ct; the old "sort ascending, take the lowest ppsf" shortcut produced distress/investor sales $100k+ below the contract):
  1. TARGETS: purchase ppsf = purchase price / GLA, (price band = tie-breaker only). Write the target down before sorting.
  2. SORT $/LivSqFt ascending, but do NOT take the bottom of the list. Work the rows whose ppsf is within about +/-15% of the target (price band is only a tie-breaker). Also read the page-1 results to see how far the lowest rows sit below the target; that gap itself is a warning sign.
  3. RECENCY/DISTANCE: prefer closings in the last 6 months and the closest distance; flag any pick older than 6 months (it needs a time adjustment). Prefer single-story if the subject is single-story; skip two-story and converted-garage listings.
  4. REMARKS FIRST: export the shortlist (type MLS#s comma-separated into the speedbar, Select All, Actions > Export > Spark) and read "Property Description" and "Private Remarks" in the CSV for every finalist before opening photos.
  5. REJECT BOTH WAYS: too nice (remodeled kitchen/baths, new LVP + granite + shaker cabinets, "completely updated") AND too distressed ("needs roof/foundation", "no repairs", "AS IS WHERE IS", investor special, flip pitch with an ARV claim) unless the subject itself was that poor. Also reject comps whose GLA includes a garage conversion/addition not in title records. Target is the subject's pre-rehab condition (typically C4: dated but livable, original or builder-grade finishes, carpet or tired flooring, no major deferred items).
  6. PHOTO CHECK each finalist (popup Photos tab: wait ~8-10s for thumbnails, scroll). Keep dated/original kitchens and baths.
  7. SANITY CHECK before export: the 3 as-is sales should bracket or sit within about 10-15% of the purchase price, and their ppsf should be near the target. If all three sit far below the contract, redo the search (do not export). Report a table: purchase ppsf target, price band, each pick's price, ppsf, % vs contract, close date, distance.
  Example (2017 Jennifer Ct, contract $375,000, GLA 1,821 => $206/sf): old picks were $240-275k ($141-151/sf, -27% to -36%); revised picks 2114 Lymington $335k (04/01/26, dated, reno-ready), 1725 Mayflower $355k (09/16/26, LVP + painted cabinets, light updates), 1733 Brighton $367k (07/15/26, vacant, dated tile/paneling) = -10.7% to -2.1% vs contract, $183-199/sf. Rejected: 3107 Windsor (remodeled kitchen), 1719 Brighton (full renovation), 2101 Deerfield (garage conversion), 3226 Delaford (two-story).
- AS-RENOVATED: sort $/LivSqFt descending (high ppsf is a proxy for quality in the submarket). Check photos closely for FULL recent renovation (kitchen, baths, flooring, paint) with materials in line with the renovation scope. Partial updates (e.g. original oak cabinets kept, mixed old/new baths) or conspicuously weak/upscale materials = reject. If none qualify, filter upward by price and repeat; if still none, relax filters (drop to radius only, widen ranges) and repeat.
- MATERIAL QUALITY BEATS PPSF/PROXIMITY for as-renovated picks. High ppsf and close distance alone are not enough: prefer comps whose finishes are visibly higher quality (custom cabinets, real counters, upgraded fixtures, quality flooring, updated baths) over flip-grade finishes (painted original cabinets, laminate/granite-look counters, basic LVP). Example (1309 London): user preferred 1111 Sheppard and 211 Piccadilly over 1112 Old Knoll and 1211 Sheppard despite the latter's higher ppsf/closer distance, and kept 1300 Wyndham. Do not reject a candidate solely for keeping some original elements if the description and photos show strong upgraded materials; when unsure, include it and flag the doubt.
- ALWAYS read the listing description/remarks for every finalist (they name the materials and renovations: cabinets, counters, flooring, appliances, baths, recent dates). Use remarks (the popup Listing tab / the Summary side panel when a row is clicked in the new UI / Customer Brief w/ Remarks display) together with photos; remarks surface upgrades photos may not show.
- MARK the 6 picks with the row checkboxes (x~17) in the new-UI grid. Marks are lost if criteria change, so export before changing filters.

- PRICE NEVER DISQUALIFIES OR DEMOTES AN AS-RENOVATED COMP (user 2026-10-16, 4404 Cleveland: 4524 Boston $589k vs $500k ARV was wrongly passed over). Rank renovated candidates ONLY by renovation completeness/quality, then recency (closed sooner is better) and proximity. Sale price or $/sf above the ARV is irrelevant and must not be a reason to skip, rank lower, or leave a comp out of the report. A recent, close, fully renovated comp beats an older one even if it sold far above the ARV. Every full-reno candidate must appear in the report (pick, alternate, or rejected with a non-price reason). Price/$/sf alignment applies to the AS-IS side only.

## B2. Classification and shortlist (mandatory every run)
1. Pull ALL pool rows (per page 100, execute_javascript) and fetch every ToFull page in-page for remarks (see MEMORY.md bulk-remarks method). Classify EVERY comp (AS-IS cand / RENO cand / REJECT + one-line reason: recency, distance, garage conversion, auction/investor, GLA oddity, condition).
2. PHOTO-VERIFY materials for every comp that survives the cheap gates (closed within ~9 months, not an obvious reject). Do NOT grade from remarks alone; remarks-only grades must be labeled "remarks only". Use the fast contact-sheet method: load `/home/polar/workflows/matrix-comp-search/photo_sheet.js` with execute_javascript code_file on the results tab (needs the results page open; defines batch2([[mls,recordId],...]) -> 12 EVENLY SPACED photos starting at index 3, so kitchens/baths show, not just exteriors). ~13 comps per call, file_path to save JSON, then stitch 3 comps per JPEG with PIL (label bar: MLS#, address, sf, price, close date, distance) and view with `read`. Far faster than opening tabs.
3. Tier every comp by materials from the photos: D distressed / C4 dated-original (oak cabinets, laminate, old baths) / C3 light refresh (paint, flooring, some counters) / mid refresh (white cabinets + granite, LVP) / full reno (new cabinets, quartz, SS, LVP, both baths - matches typical RW scope) / upscale-custom.
4. Write a SHORTLIST of the legit candidates (top ~5-10, however many truly qualify) with tier, key materials, price/sf, date, distance, and which side (as-is vs as-improved) each serves. It must show how the shortlist BRACKETS quality: as-is side vs the subject's as-is condition (low/mid/high of what is available) and as-improved side vs the subject's renovation scope (lower-quality, equal, above). State where the subject's as-is and as-improved states fall inside the bracket, and flag if the pool cannot bracket (e.g. no true C4 comps inside the price band).
5. Pick the 3+3 FROM the shortlist; explain why each beat near-misses. Write report (md): targets, method, shortlist + bracket table, per-comp terse table for ALL comps with a column "verified by: photos / remarks only", thesis, alternates, caveats. File to OneDrive comps/ as comp_pool_accounting__<slug>.md (and shortlist as comp_shortlist__<slug>.md if separate). Never overwrite.

## B3. Selection logic corrections (user review of 312 Timber Ridge Ln, 2026-10-13) - APPLY EVERY RUN
1. AS-RENOVATED = the MOST FULLY IMPROVED comps, distance and price secondary ("distance be damned"). Rank by completeness and quality of the renovation: kitchen, ALL baths, flooring throughout, roof/systems/foundation/windows. A close comp with a PARTIAL renovation (e.g. 900 Tupelo: some carpet left, partial baths) ranks BELOW a farther comp with a complete, higher-quality one (336 Lakewood: new roof, foundation work, quartz, remodeled baths; 150 Edgewood: remodeled kitchen with butcher block, tile shower). Do not exclude a full-reno comp for being 1.5-2 mi away or priced well above contract. Proximity breaks ties only.
2. AS-IS = align with the purchase $/sf (price / GLA), not just the nearest dated houses. When the pool has dated C4 comps both near the contract $/sf and +20-25% above it, prefer the ones near the contract $/sf (312 Timber Ridge: 508 Grace $218/sf and 224 Willow $207/sf over 306 Pepperwood / 448 Woodway at $230-304/sf). Keep 309 Pepperwood-type same-street dated comps.
3. Reject for condition ONLY if C5 or C6 per the UAD/USPAP definitions: C5 = obvious deferred maintenance, significant repairs/rehab needed; C6 = substantial damage or deferred maintenance, severe, may not be livable. Dated/worn/original finishes with no deferred damage = C4 and ACCEPTABLE as-is. State the C-rating for every as-is candidate from photos.
4. Do NOT auto-reject low-priced dated comps, receivership/REO sales, or duplex-remarked listings just for that. Evaluate actual condition against C5/C6 (receivership sales that are merely dated are C4). A duplex-remarked listing coded Single Family Residence and fee simple can be used; FLAG it and ask the user to confirm it is a separate fee-simple unit. Still reject true attached/half-duplex with shared title, 2 BR, or two-story when the subject is one story.
5. A 4th renovated or an alternate is fine to leave checked; the user trims. Show both sides (as-is by $/sf, renovated by completeness) in the report, and list what the picks assume.
6. PROXIMITY + BRACKETING STILL MATTER for the renovated set (user's final call on 312 Timber Ridge: kept 900 Tupelo, dropped 102 Pinyon). A renovated comp that is very close (0.2 mi) and clearly renovated is a good pick even if a bit less complete, because it anchors the local market; fullness wins over it only for the TOP quality slot. Aim for the set to BRACKET the subject's as-renovated state: include (a) the fullest/most upscale reno (336 Lakewood), (b) a strong reno (150 Edgewood) and (c) a close, solid-but-less-than-top reno (900 Tupelo) so TrueTracts adjusts both up and down. Do NOT pick a lowest-cost reno merely because its price/scope looks like the subject (102 Pinyon, $350k) when it would sit below the bracket.
7. The final set is always the USER's checked grid set. Re-read it every time (they re-edit after export), re-export as the next __N, and never assume an earlier export is final. Order for TrueTracts: renovated 1-3 ascending by sale price, then as-is ascending by sale price.
8. Explain in the report, for every pick, WHY it beat near-misses in these terms: reno completeness, closeness, $/sf vs contract, C-rating. Surface the judgment calls (partial-vs-close, duplex-coded) as questions rather than silently excluding.
